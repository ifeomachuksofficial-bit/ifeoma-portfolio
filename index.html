Product Marketing & Behavioral Analytics

Bellabeat Smart-Device Usage Analysis

Turning Wearable Telemetry into Actionable Product Marketing Insights

Tools: BigQuery SQL, Tableau, Spreadsheets, Exploratory Analysis
Dataset: Fitbit Fitness Tracker Telemetry (33 Users, 940 Daily Obs.)
Role: Product Marketing Data Analyst
Framework: Ask → Prepare → Process → Analyze → Share → Act

01. Business Objective

Bellabeat products combine smart wellness tracking with a specialized focus on women’s health. The goal was not simply calculating average steps, but examining consumer usage patterns to identify meaningful behavioral segments that inform Bellabeat’s app marketing, personalized reminders, and feature engagement strategy.

02. Data Audit & Processing

Audited daily tracking records from April 12 – May 12, 2016. Joined Daily Steps (940 rows, 33 users) and Daily Intensities (940 rows, 33 users) in Google BigQuery using unique ID and ActivityDay, engineering active ratios and quality flags.

03 & 04. Data Quality Sensitivity Analysis: Does Cleaning Matter?

The daily dataset contained 77 zero-step records. Rather than arbitrarily deleting records, an analytical rule identified Possible Non-Wear records where total_steps = 0 AND total_active_minutes = 0 AND sedentary_minutes = 1440 (72 records). A sensitivity audit proved this decision materially changes behavioral baselines:

Metric All Observations (940 Rows) Excluding Possible Non-Wear (868 Rows) Material Impact (% Delta)
Average Daily Steps 7,637.91 8,271.47 +8.3% increase
Average Active Minutes 227.54 min 246.42 min +8.3% increase
Average Sedentary Minutes 991.21 min (16.5 hrs) 953.98 min (15.9 hrs) -3.8% decrease

Methodological Takeaway: Data cleaning is part of the analytical result, not a cosmetic step before it. Unfiltered master tables were preserved for traceability while filtered data was utilized for behavioral segmentation.

User-Level Step Distribution

Average daily steps ranged from 1,578 → 16,040 across the 33 users. Sample quartiles constructed relative behavioral tiers:

  • Q1 (Lower): 5,566.87 steps/day
  • Median: 7,555.77 steps/day (Mean: 7,886.42)
  • Q3 (Upper): 9,684.17 steps/day
  • Middle 50% Range: 5,567 – 9,684 steps/day

Behavioral Relationship Correlations

Statistical correlations revealed critical disconnects between tracking and movement:

  • Tracking Coverage ↔ Steps (r = 0.143): Weak positive linear relationship. Tracking frequently does not mean a user is physically active.
  • Steps ↔ Sedentary Minutes (r = -0.258): Only weakly negative. Active users still accumulate high sedentary time.
  • Steps ↔ Active Minutes (r = +0.675): Moderately strong positive linear relationship.

Product Marketing Recommendations

  • Differentiate Sedentary vs. Movement Messaging: Because active minutes and sedentary time correlate weakly (r = -0.258), test periodic daytime inactivity breaks rather than pushing daily step goals on users who already complete workouts.
  • Segmented In-App Campaigns: Users who track consistently but record low steps need micro-challenges and progressive habit rewards, rather than aggressive fitness targets.
  • Weekend Consistency Challenges: The 1,455-step gap between Saturday mean and median indicates high variance; test weekend consistency reminders on Friday afternoons.

Methodological Constraints & Limitations

  • Small Sample Size: 33 total participants over a 1-month window (2016). Results represent directional behavioral hypotheses, not population-level conclusions.
  • Demographic Blindspots: Dataset lacks gender, age, and lifestyle indicators; cannot verify whether participants mirror Bellabeat’s female-focused target market.
  • Correlation vs. Causation: Observable relationships describe associations within the sample, requiring experimental A/B validation before full GTM rollout.
