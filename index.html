About Me

I spent seven years in marketing learning how to make people pay attention to words, only to realise I was becoming increasingly curious about the numbers people left behind.

A campaign would end. The report would say engagement increased by 32%. Everyone would celebrate, close the report and move on to the next campaign.

Meanwhile, I’d still be staring at the 32%.

Why did it increase? Who responded? What changed? Was it the message, the audience, the timing—or something we hadn’t even considered?

That curiosity is what pulled me into data analytics.

Today, I use Excel, Google Sheets and SQL to clean data, investigate patterns and answer business questions. But I haven’t left my marketing brain behind, and I don’t intend to. Seven years of thinking about audiences taught me that there is usually a person behind every click, conversion and percentage—and context behind every number.

So when I look at a dataset, I’m not interested in producing a pretty chart and calling it a day. I want to know what happened, why it happened, what we can reasonably learn from it and, most importantly, what someone should do with that information.

Maybe that’s the marketer in me. Maybe that’s the analyst.

I think it’s useful to be both.

I like numbers, but I love what they mean—and I’m happiest somewhere between the spreadsheet and the sentence that finally makes it all make sense.

Case Study 01 · Behavioral Telemetry

Bellabeat Smart-Device Usage Analysis

Turning Wearable-Device Behavior into Actionable Product Marketing Insights

Tools: BigQuery SQL, Tableau, Spreadsheets, Exploratory Analysis
Dataset: Fitbit Telemetry (33 Users, 940 Daily Observations)
Role: Product Marketing Data Analyst
Framework: Ask → Prepare → Process → Analyze → Share → Act

01. Business Task

Bellabeat manufactures high-end wellness trackers tailored for women. The objective was not merely calculating average step counts, but analyzing customer engagement telemetry to determine how consumers track activity, identify behavioral friction, and translate those findings into personalized in-app marketing campaigns.

02. Data Processing in BigQuery

Joined daily steps and intensity tables (940 observations across 33 participants) in Google BigQuery. Computed user-level aggregate baselines (mean, median, observation coverage %, sedentary minutes) so participants with 31 days would not disproportionately skew comparisons.

03 & 04. Data Cleaning Sensitivity Audit

The daily dataset contained 77 zero-step records. Rather than arbitrarily deleting records, a conservative quality rule flagged Possible Non-Wear when total_steps = 0 AND total_active_minutes = 0 AND sedentary_minutes = 1440 (72 records). A sensitivity test proved cleaning is part of the analytical result:

Metric All Observations (940 Rows) Excluding Possible Non-Wear (868 Rows) Material Impact (% Delta)
Average Daily Steps 7,637.91 8,271.47 +8.3% increase
Average Active Minutes 227.54 min 246.42 min +8.3% increase
Average Sedentary Minutes 991.21 min (16.5 hrs) 953.98 min (15.9 hrs) -3.8% decrease

Tableau Portfolio Visual

Scatterplot mapping Observation Coverage (%) against Average Daily Steps across all 33 participants[cite: 6]. 1. High Activity + High Coverage: Product power users; prime candidates for premium coaching tiers.
2. High Activity + Low Coverage: Naturally active; benefit from automated passive sync reminders.
3. Low Activity + High Coverage: Highly engaged app habit; requires micro-challenges and progressive goals.
4. Low Activity + Low Coverage: High-risk churn segment; requires immediate onboarding intervention.

Product Marketing Recommendations

  • Separate Inactivity from Workout Reminders: Because steps and sedentary time correlate weakly ($r = -0.258$), prompt daytime posture breaks rather than generic step pushes.
  • Segmented Micro-Goals: Target users who track frequently but record low steps with gradual 15-minute walking goals.
  • Friday Habit Nudges: Mitigate the high Saturday variance by testing pre-weekend consistency reminders.

Methodological Constraints

  • Sample Constraints: 33 participants across a 30-day window (2016); directional hypotheses rather than population-wide baselines.
  • Demographic Blindspots: Lacks demographic segmentation to prove participants represent Bellabeat’s female customer base.
  • Correlation ≠ Causation: Observable relationships describe historical associations requiring controlled A/B testing before feature release.

Data Preparation & Google Sheets Formulas

Deduplicated 10 raw rows, normalized mixed-case issue strings using =PROPER(TRIM()), and verified timestamp formats[cite: 4]. Enriched individual ticket records by pulling transaction metadata from the separate Orders table via exact-match VLOOKUP[cite: 4]: 

=IF($B2="","",VLOOKUP($B2, Orders!$A$2:$L$2401, 8, FALSE))

Resolution duration was engineered as (resolved_at - created_at) * 24[cite: 4]. SLA benchmarks were dynamically assigned by priority tier (Urgent: 4h, High: 12h, Normal: 24h, Low: 48h) to evaluate breach rates against operational targets[cite: 4].

1. Post-Purchase Fulfillment Burden

Delivery Delays (1,211 tickets; 40.5%) and Damaged Items (467 tickets; 15.6%) generated over 56% of total inbound volume[cite: 4]. Post-purchase friction consumed customer support capacity, rather than pre-sale discovery questions[cite: 4].

2. Channel Efficiency & CSAT Divergence

Email proved to be the slowest channel (111 min first response; 25.8h resolution)[cite: 4]. Conversely, Live Chat achieved the fastest resolution (15.5h) and highest satisfaction score (3.66/5.0)[cite: 4], proving synchronous resolution preserves customer retention.

Support Strategy & Retention Interventions

  • Automated Self-Service Tracking: Deploy proactive SMS order tracking updates to deflect up to 30% of "Where is my order?" delivery delay tickets.
  • Channel Routing & Reallocation: Route high-urgency refund requests away from email queues toward Live Chat to reduce resolution latency.
  • Packaging Quality Review: High return volumes in fragile categories require vendor packaging audits to reduce damaged item claims at the source.

Key Analytical Observations

  • Slashed Blank-Slate Friction: Replacing a passive 4-slide tour with a role-based setup checklist lowered time-to-first-project from 28.4 to 6.2 minutes, unlocking immediate activation.
  • No Quality Dilution: Paid upgrades grew by +62.5% without depressing ARPU ($5.15 vs. $3.00), demonstrating stronger user intent.
  • Device Parity: Desktop activation reached 15.04% and mobile reached 15.64%, proving the lightweight checklist avoided small-screen usability drops.

GTM Rollout Recommendations

  • 100% Production Rollout: Fully decommission the legacy modal walkthrough across all signup channels to capture +$136.5K incremental ARR.
  • Triggered Lifecycle Emails: Implement automated behavioral re-engagement emails for users who complete Step 1 but stall before Step 2 within 6 hours.
  • Mobile App Alignment: Maintain checklist parity across mobile apps to prevent cross-platform conversion leakage.
